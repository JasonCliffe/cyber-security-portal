Password security

Reducing the damage one stolen password can cause.

Why weak passwords are a problem

Attackers often reuse stolen passwords across many services, hoping people have used the same password in more than one place. If one of your accounts is breached and you reuse that password elsewhere, they may gain access to several services at once.

Strong password habits

  • Use at least 12 characters – Longer passphrases are much harder to guess.
  • Keep passwords unique – A different password for each important account.
  • Use a password manager – Let software handle generating and storing complex passwords.
  • Avoid personal information – Don't use names, birthdays or obvious words that someone could guess from social media.

Examples of weak passwords

These are easy for attackers to guess or find in leaked password lists.

  • 123456
  • password
  • welcome1
  • qwerty
  • summer2024

Making strong passwords manageable

You don't need to memorise dozens of complex strings. Focus on a few key steps instead.

  1. Choose a long passphrase for your main email account — for example, three random words with numbers or punctuation.
  2. Turn on multi‑factor authentication wherever it's offered, especially for email, banking and important work systems.
  3. Use a reputable password manager to generate and store unique passwords for other sites and apps.
  4. If you hear that a service you use has been breached, change the password there and anywhere else you reused it.

If you think a password has been stolen

  1. Change the password on that service immediately.
  2. Change passwords on other services where you used the same or a similar password.
  3. Turn on multi‑factor authentication on the affected accounts.
  4. Watch for unexpected sign‑in alerts or activity and report anything suspicious.

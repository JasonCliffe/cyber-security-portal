Safe browsing

Checking websites and downloads before you trust them.

Why safe browsing matters

Many attacks start with a visit to a malicious or compromised website, or by downloading a file from an untrusted source. A few simple checks can reduce the chance of installing malware or entering details on a fake site.

Safe browsing checklist

  • Only visit websites you trust or have found through reputable sources.
  • Keep your browser, operating system and apps updated.
  • Avoid downloading software from pop‑ups or random links.
  • Use multi‑factor authentication where possible for important online accounts.
  • Verify that the web address looks right before entering any sensitive information.

What to look for in the browser

  • Check the URL starts with https:// when entering passwords or payment details. This indicates that the connection is encrypted.
  • Look for a closed padlock icon and click it if you want to see more information about the site's certificate.
  • Watch out for small spelling changes in addresses, such as paypa1.com instead of paypal.com.

Downloading files safely

  1. Prefer official app stores or the organisation's own website.
  2. Be cautious of “free” tools offered through ads or pop‑ups.
  3. Scan downloaded files with trusted security software before opening them.
  4. If you're unsure whether a file is needed, check with your IT team or the person who asked you to install it.

If you think you've visited a malicious site

  1. Close the tab and browser.
  2. Run a security scan on your device.
  3. Change passwords for any accounts you accessed shortly before or after the visit.
  4. Report the incident if it relates to work systems or sensitive data.
